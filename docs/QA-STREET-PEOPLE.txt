STREET ACTOR DEPTH

There were two distinct failures, in addition to the separate walking-route
problem:

* At night the screen-effects layer drew 12 human-shaped decorations at
  three fixed intersections. They were not pedestrians. Each group moved
  within a 31-unit band with an abrupt wrap and could be placed on the road.
  Switching to Low FX could make the four people disappear. This layer
  now draws only lighting/weather, not human figures.
* The chase view represented a live pedestrian using three upright solid
  rectangles. These were not literal ground polygons, but had no legs,
  posture, or contact shadow. The same foot locations now have boots,
  jointed legs and arms, torso thickness, and a ground-contact shadow.
  Cars/buildings and pedestrians use the same chase depth buffer.
* In overhead view, the original actor sprite stays at the same size; the
  soft contact shadow is painted before bodies. Nearby facades clip just the
  hidden sprite pixels, rather than re-stamping an unlit wall over other
  objects. Downed combat actors remain low and distinct; they no longer
  appear as a diagonal line. No dialogue, movement or combat values changed.

Verified on an isolated Linux workspace: native SDL fresh-door/venue replay,
the deterministic depth geometry fixture, standing/stride and downed poses,
fixed height and foot location, near-wall occlusion, and a zero-NPC night
frame without ghost crowds. With the companion outer-walker implementation,
both views render the same persistent shoulder walkers and leave their
routes/positions untouched. The view still has intentionally stylized arcade
geometry; no real Windows desktop run was used for these checks.
